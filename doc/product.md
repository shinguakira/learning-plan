# What this is for

Learning Plan is a study planner for one person teaching themselves software
engineering.

## The problem it addresses

Anyone learning this field ends up with a list of things they "should" learn that
grows faster than they can work through it. React, SQL, Docker, networking,
algorithms, a certification, a portfolio project. The list is not the hard part —
the hard part is that a list gives you no way to answer three questions:

- **What should I do next?** Everything looks equally urgent when it is a flat list.
- **Am I actually moving?** Without a record of what is finished, months of effort
  feel the same as no effort.
- **Have I planned more than fits?** Five things "in progress" at once is not a
  plan, it is five things that will all take four times as long.

Most people fall back on a notes app or a spreadsheet. Those hold the list, but
they do not show time, so the third question stays invisible until the deadlines
have already been missed.

## What you do with it

**Write down what you intend to learn.** Each item gets a start date, a deadline,
a rough hour estimate, and a note saying what "finished" means — not "learn SQL"
but "read EXPLAIN output and tune three slow queries." A goal you cannot tell you
have reached is a goal you will never close.

**Move things along as you go.** One click cycles an item between not started, in
progress, and done. That is the whole ceremony; anything heavier and you stop
doing it.

**See the plan as a list when you are working.** Sort by deadline to see what is
next, or by priority when you have an hour and want it to count. Filter to one
subject when you want to focus, or search when you half-remember writing something
down.

**See the plan on a calendar when you are planning.** The same items, drawn as bars
across the months, with a line on today. This is the view that answers the third
question: overlapping bars are how you find out you have committed to four subjects
in the same fortnight, before the fortnight arrives. Bars behind today's line that
are not finished are the things quietly slipping.

**Watch the progress figure.** How much is done, how much is left, how many
deadlines have already passed, and how many hours you have committed to in total.
The overdue count is deliberately prominent — a plan with no overdue items is
usually a plan nobody is being honest about.

**Ask questions without leaving.** A separate chat page answers the things that come
up mid-study — how to approach a topic, why something behaves the way it does,
what to learn first. It has no knowledge of your task list and does not try to
manage your plan for you; it is there so that hitting a wall does not mean opening
five browser tabs and losing the thread.

## Who it is for

Someone studying largely alone, over months rather than weeks, with real deadlines
somewhere ahead — a certification date, a job search, a career change. It assumes
you are the one deciding what to learn. It does not supply a curriculum, and it is
not a course.

It is single-user by design. Nothing is shared, nothing is submitted, nobody else
sees it. Nothing is stored either: the plan lives only for as long as the page is
open, so this is somewhere to think a plan through rather than somewhere to keep it.

## What it deliberately is not

- **Not a habit tracker.** No streaks, no daily targets, no guilt mechanics.
  Missing a day is not the failure mode being addressed here; planning badly is.
- **Not a team tool.** No assignees, no comments, no sharing, no permissions.
- **Not a curriculum.** Sample items ship with it as an illustration of the level
  of detail that works, not as a syllabus to follow.
- **Not a general to-do app.** Everything is shaped around subjects, estimates and
  deadlines that stretch across months. Groceries do not belong in it.

## How to tell it is working

You open the timeline before committing to something new, and occasionally decide
not to. The overdue count goes down because you re-dated things honestly rather
than because you deleted them. When someone asks what you have been learning, you
can answer with specifics instead of a shrug.
